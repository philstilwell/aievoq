Common dynamics / 2

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI development also involves combining methods, finding new uses for existing abilities and relying on other systems. Outcomes depend on those relationships and on earlier decisions. A model's own capabilities explain only part of its success or failure.

How creatures change one another's prospects

Some parallels concern evolution across generations; others concern ecological relationships. We will name which process a story actually shows.

Shared dynamicIn living systemsIn AI development
7. RepurposingAn existing feature can acquire a new use: biologists call this exaptation.Capabilities developed in one setting can be recruited for another. We should trace the original role and the later use. [64], [68]
8. Cooperation and dependencePartners can gain from exchanging services, while becoming vulnerable to their loss.Models, tools and people can form useful partnerships. Their connections bring both reach and dependencies. [54], [55], [42], [43]
9. Reciprocal adaptationA change in one population can alter pressures on another, inviting a response.Opposing agents in a hide-and-seek experiment developed successive counter-strategies. Industry-wide arms races need their own evidence. [59], [67]
10. Habitat constructionOrganisms alter surroundings and sometimes the conditions shaping future selection.AI use can change workflows and the skills people practice, changing what becomes useful next. That feedback is a reporting question. [58], [59], [44], [46]
11. Branching and specializationRelated populations can diverge; some divergences eventually produce distinct species.A shared model can support distinct adaptations. Durable differences in role may justify our provisional species portraits. [62], [64], [69]
12. Historical contingencyEarlier changes can make later innovations possible; another history might take another route.Available components and inherited designs can shape what is feasible next. Present arrangements need not be inevitable or optimal. [66], [69]

Two further clues: similar functions can arise along different paths, suggesting convergence; disappearance from a habitat can suggest replacement. Both require a history. Similarity does not prove independent origin, and an unavailable product may leave reusable descendants behind.
