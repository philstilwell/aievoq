The growing appetite for AI

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Demand for AI has several signs: current use, intended future use, willingness to pay and investment in capacity. These measures describe different decisions. Business surveys help establish how widely AI is used without proving that every user pays or that every use is productive.

The watering hole is busy. Some visitors take a cautious sip; others arrive with buckets. A few are already proposing a larger watering hole.

The Census Bureau's October 22 release provides a timely sighting: 24.6% of U.S. businesses with employees, excluding agriculture, report recent AI use in a business function; 28.6% expect to use it within six months. These estimates measure use and expectations, not paying customers or next spring's outcome. [70]

The appetite is broad, but uneven. Two research teams help explain the picture. A Census study finds that adoption often reaches only a few business functions. A separate survey of workers finds AI spread across many occupations, while remaining less common within particular tasks. A creature can enter many habitats without taking over every activity inside them. [71], [72]

This substantial foothold leaves room for further adoption. It does not establish equal intensity of use or provider profit. These American survey findings are not worldwide market shares.
