The borrowed exoskeleton

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Assisted performance is what someone can accomplish while using AI. Retained learning is what they understand or can do afterward, including without assistance. An experiment must test these separately before better immediate output can be treated as evidence of lasting skill.

A borrowed shell can widen the territory a creature can enter. What happens when the shell comes off? October's research publications give that question a practical human counterpart.

A study published in Nature Human Behaviour on October 26 tested 487 management consultants on unfamiliar data-science work. AI access and training improved performance on three tasks, but did not bring the consultants to the expert benchmark. The researchers found no evidence of better answers to technical questions afterwards without AI. This is a new journal publication of work that had circulated earlier. [84]

Another route beyond familiar territory

An independent October 2026 working paper studied 673 final-year IT apprentices at 12 German schools. AI access raised the share of available marks earned by 26 and 31 percentage points on two tasks beyond their formal curriculum. It found no reduction in immediate task comprehension. The experiment ran from October 2025 to January 2026; the paper is not yet peer reviewed. [85]

What can change?What these studies leave open
Reach with assistanceBetter performance on unfamiliar tasks is possible. That does not establish professional expertise.
Understanding without itResults differ across tasks and tests. Immediate comprehension is not the same as durable learning.
A worker's prospectsNeither study measures jobs saved, lasting pay gains or long-term career security.

The insight: ask whether the partnership extends a person's reach, develops their own abilities, or does both. The studies support assisted performance in different settings; they do not settle long-term learning in either direction. For laborans, the most useful field test has two parts: work with the partner, then check what can still be explained and done alone. [84], [85]
