The field journal / 2 min read

More features for the field journal

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI's effects extend beyond its own task scores to human learning, organizational choices and reliance on supporting systems. Recurring investigations can follow those relationships over time. Each needs a concrete question and its own evidence rather than an assumed overall benefit or harm.

A recurring cast needs recurring encounters

The Food Web and the Nursery

Draw dependencies in both directions: what supports the AI, and what people now rely on it to do. Add a loss-of-function ledger for delegated skills and a nursery check for newcomers' learning. The Relay Octopus's cord can represent a useful partnership or a vulnerability to investigate; its appearance alone does not decide which.

The Fossil Cabinet

Give a retired system a short obituary: what it once did well, what displaced it, and what survived in descendants or other products. Preserve the old portrait. A local disappearance receives a local label, as the October 14 entry illustrates.

The Naturalist Was Wrong

Now introduced in this issue: distinguish a documented prediction from a reader's assumption, follow October's surprises, and preserve future expectations before their outcomes arrive. Print the original beside the result. The ledger applies to our own judgments as well as those of prominent figures.

Two Creatures, One Task

Follow two systems through the same practical job. Use small illustrated scenes, an honest account of assistance, and a short result table. Choose an encounter that exposes a meaningful difference rather than an arbitrary winner.

Who Benefits from the Adaptation?

Ask which users, workers, researchers, or institutions gain from a new ability, and who must adjust. The mathematical disruption is the first major example. A successful AI can improve one activity while unsettling the community around it.

The Mimicry Watch

Examine cases in which the appearance of competence outruns the evidence: fluent explanations, impressive-looking documents, or unreliable demonstrations. Include the test that exposed the difference. This feature needs particular care to avoid treating one failure as the creature's whole identity.

Now introduced: Beyond the Glasshouse, The Price of Feathers, and The Habitat Engineers have dedicated field-note pages with reporting slots. Letters from the Undergrowth begins with an explicitly fictional Octopus dispatch. Species passports, molting plates and further encounters remain available as the quarter supplies suitable stories. Rotate departments rather than require every one in every issue.
