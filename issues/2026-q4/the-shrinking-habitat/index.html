Humans, learning & work / 2 min read

The shrinking habitat

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI can substitute for some human tasks and complement others. Its effects on paid work depend on employers' decisions, changing demand, access and training as well as workers' choices. The working-human feature examines both displacement and useful human-AI partnerships.

Homo sapiens laborans / the working human

Meet Homo sapiens laborans: familiar face, useful hands, and a livelihood that depends on finding work worth doing. Laborans is a playful field-guide name for the working human, not a scientific subspecies. The habitat is the world of paid tasks, employers, customers and opportunities.

Two arrangements, one human

Left / Stepping back. The person hands over the work and withdraws from its decisions. The Octopus occupies the bench; the notebook stays closed. This imagined scene asks what happens when delegation includes surrendering the ability to judge what comes back.

Right / Working in partnership. The same person puts the Octopus's capabilities to use, checks the result and chooses the next step. The tools extend the worker's reach while the worker remains involved. Whether this brings lasting advantage needs evidence.

These are possible working arrangements, not two kinds of human. A person can move between them. Delegation can also be a sensible way to free time for other work. Job loss may follow decisions made elsewhere, even when someone has adapted well.

Where the metaphor bends: workers can lose demand for their skills without losing the skills themselves. Their choices matter, alongside access to tools, training, bargaining power and employers' decisions. A shrinking niche is no verdict on a person's worth.
