The evolutionary lens / 2 min read

Welcome to the AI jungle

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI systems use trained models to produce answers or choose actions. Their usefulness also depends on tools, access, price and human decisions. Following AI therefore means tracking both what the technology can do and the conditions that let people use it.

Every jungle has an electricity bill

Imagine arriving in a jungle where the inhabitants are redesigned every few weeks, the food supply is metered, and the keepers sometimes remove an animal because its younger cousin is cheaper to feed. The animals have release notes. The weather has subscription tiers.

This journal follows the creatures through a changing season. Some acquire useful new tricks. Some find a neglected niche. Others discover that yesterday's impressive performance has become today's ordinary expectation. Around them, people rearrange the habitat: connecting tools, changing prices, granting access, or taking it away.

Our first issue follows a particularly consequential migration: AI into the production of mathematical research. The resulting upheaval involves much more than difficult puzzles. It touches the way people discover, check, understand, and share knowledge. Those questions will travel with AI into other fields.

Three invented creatures will guide us. The Ledgerfinch stands for economical, repeated information work. The Relay Octopus represents an assistant reaching into tools and outside services. The Proofweaver accompanies the story of mathematical discovery. Homo sapiens laborans, the working human, joins them to explore displacement and partnership.

The creatures make functions memorable; the statistics let us test the story. A recurring census follows adoption, employment, capability, operating prices and energy. Each measure keeps its population, date and comparison visible. A new feather is an illustration; a measured improvement needs evidence.

A few things to keep in the field bag

A model is the underlying AI. A working system adds tools, information, and permissions. A product packages these for people to use. Their fortunes can differ: a product may gain a function without its model changing, or a model may disappear from one product while remaining available elsewhere.

Broad conclusions need more than one source. We draw on research papers, show disagreement, and distinguish findings from our own ecological metaphors. Company claims stay attributed. The longer source trail is at the back, so the story can keep moving.

The question for this quarter: which creatures are becoming more useful, and how are their successes changing the world around them?
