Beyond the Glasshouse

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI performance in a controlled test can differ from performance in daily work. Instructions may conflict, files may change and important information may be missing. Testing whether the system still completes the task reveals how well its abilities carry into less predictable conditions.

Can the creature manage an ordinary Tuesday?

In the glasshouse, every drawer has a label and the weather keeps its appointment. Outside, someone has renamed the folder, the instructions disagree, and the person who knows the answer has gone to lunch. This recurring feature will follow a familiar AI creature across that boundary.

Research on agent evaluation identifies weak tests and overlooked costs; research on longer software tasks examines sustained task completion and its limits. Together they give us reasons to look beyond a single impressive performance. Neither study supplies a verdict on October's workplace products. [40], [39]

Reserved encounter / the Octopus leaves the demonstration

Give one documented system the same modest information task in two settings. First provide a tidy set of files. Then introduce a realistic complication, such as a missing source or conflicting versions. Keep a record of the system, instructions, tools and human help. Repeat the encounter before drawing a general conclusion.

What the reader will see: what changed, what the creature tried, whether the result was usable, and how much rescuing it needed. A request for clarification may be the sensible response.

Reporting status: encounter design only. No test has been run and no success rate is assigned. The imagined comparison is pictured above; observed results remain to be collected.

Where the metaphor bends: a glasshouse is our image for controlled conditions. It does not mean that every demonstration is misleading or that every workplace is hostile.
