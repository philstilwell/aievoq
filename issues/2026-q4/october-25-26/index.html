Mathematics & the habitat · October 25-26 / 2 min read

Who gets to inspect the web?

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Access to AI includes access to systems, their outputs and the material needed to check claims about them. Publication policies and operating prices affect who can participate. These access decisions can change how widely a capability benefits researchers or other users.

The mathematical dispute continued. On October 25, the independent advisory group argued against companies choosing particular mathematicians to lead the understanding and presentation of their output. It raised concerns about unequal access, influence over research priorities, and the use of scholars' reputations. On October 26, it called for prompt public release of material behind announced claims in mathematical physics. These are the group's stated concerns and recommendations. [17]

The future-facing question is plain: if powerful AI systems produce important ideas, who gets to examine them, develop them, and decide what they mean? Access to the new animal can become power over the surrounding habitat.

October 26 / Another arrival at the feeding ground

OpenAI recorded GPT-6.1 Terra's rollout. Its published prices join a landscape in which the cost of using an AI can shape where it is useful. [7]

The billing unit, a token, is a small piece of text. Different systems may need different amounts of text and different numbers of attempts to finish the same job. The chart compares unit prices only; it does not establish which system is the better bargain.

The useful eventual measure: how much money, time, and checking were needed to obtain a result someone could actually use?
