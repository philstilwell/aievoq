The evolutionary lens / 2 min read

Where the analogy earns its keep

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

An AI system can change in several ways: its answer can change during a conversation, its model can receive further training, or its tools and access can change. Distinguishing these levels lets us identify what actually improved and what evidence would demonstrate it.

Four distinctions that make the jungle more revealing

A lifetime is different from a lineage. An assistant changing its answer after feedback shows adjustment during use. Further training changes a model. Evolutionary change concerns what is retained and transmitted across a population or succession of designs. These can interact, as Population Based Training illustrates, but they should not be counted as the same event. [60], [63]

A market is a habitat with decision-makers. Biological evolution has no foresight or promised destination. AI developers can pursue explicit goals, while funding, distribution, rules and user choices shape which designs continue. We must investigate those choices rather than attribute every winner to technical superiority. Cultural evolution supplies a framework for examining human transmission; it does not make commercial outcomes inevitable. [61], [66]

A model, a system and a company have different family histories. Reusing learned settings, learning from another model and borrowing a research idea are different relationships. Connecting a calculator adds a component to a system. It does not, by itself, show genetic-style inheritance. Our species are functional characters; actual ancestry needs a documented development trail. [64], [65], [42], [43]

Fitness is not a moral verdict or a universal intelligence score. In biology it concerns contribution to future generations in a setting. For AI, continued use, copying and investment are possible measures of persistence; reliable, economical work is a separate outcome. We should measure both before explaining either. Growth can reward a feature we would prefer to discourage. [60], [40]

Chance also belongs in the story. Biological evolution includes random changes in the frequency of inherited variants, called genetic drift. In AI, chance discoveries or copying fashions may matter, but the label is only useful if we can identify the mechanism. The bacterial experiment in [69] illustrates a related lesson: earlier changes can affect which innovations become possible.

What the analogy buys us: follow inheritance as well as novelty; dependencies as well as abilities; and changing surroundings as well as benchmark scores. It makes no automatic prediction of consciousness, independent reproduction, endless improvement or a single species taking over the forest.
