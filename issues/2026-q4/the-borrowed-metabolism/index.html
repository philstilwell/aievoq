Humans, learning & work / 2 min read

The borrowed metabolism

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI dependence arises when people or organizations rely on an outside system for an important function. Delegation can improve efficiency, while making interruption or loss of access more costly. Dependence and skill loss are different questions and should be tested separately.

When a helpful neighbour becomes essential

A microbe does not receive a medal for making everything itself. In the right surroundings, depending on a neighbour can pay.

The Black Queen hypothesis describes how microbes may lose costly functions when neighbouring organisms supply their benefits. Separate experiments with engineered bacteria found that partners exchanging essential nutrients could gain a growth advantage over self-sufficient cells under the tested conditions. [54], [55] Dependence can accompany success. The price is that the missing function must still be supplied somewhere.

Our AI analogy is an organization that stops maintaining a capability because an assistant reliably supplies it. That might be sensible specialization. It might also leave the organization unable to recognize a bad result or cope when access changes. Which outcome occurs is a question for observation.

The analogy has a boundary: genes lost across microbial generations are different from human skills that are never acquired, become rusty, or are deliberately left to a tool. People can retrain and institutions can redesign work. Calling a skill "vestigial" would describe our metaphor, not establish a biological process.

The human side has its own research history. Bainbridge's 1983 analysis warned that automation could rely on experienced operators' knowledge while reducing opportunities to acquire or maintain it. Later AI research found that requiring people to think through a decision could reduce excessive reliance on incorrect advice, though it did not eliminate the problem. [56], [57]

The Relay Octopus's cord now carries a second question. We already ask what the AI depends on. We should also ask what its users have come to depend on it for. A partnership can be highly capable while one partner loses options.
