Could it survive a change of weather?

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI adaptability is the ability to remain useful when conditions change, such as higher costs, unfamiliar inputs or loss of a tool. It must be assessed for a specified task and setting. The amount of human rescue needed is part of that assessment.

Fitness needs an address

A creature can be excellent in one habitat and awkward in another. For AI, the habitat might be a busy support desk, an offline archive, a research group, or a workplace full of changing files.

Our eventual survival profile should answer a few ordinary questions:

QuestionWhat we would observe
Does it do useful work?How often the result meets a clearly stated standard.
Can it handle a surprise?What happens when a familiar part of the task changes.
Can it recover?Whether feedback leads to a successful correction.
Is it economical?Total money, time, and human checking per usable result.
What does it depend on?What happens when an expected tool or connection is absent.
Does the partnership renew its skills?Whether people learn, retain judgment, and can use a tested fallback.
Why choose it over a rival?Its advantage on the same jobs under comparable conditions.

These questions remain unmeasured in this draft. We will not turn them into decorative scores before collecting evidence. A survival profile must say what was tested and where; it cannot promise adaptability to every possible change.

Proposed recurring feature: A Change in the Weather. Move one menu item, change a file format, remove a connection, or tighten the budget. Follow the familiar creature through the disturbance. Show what it did, what help it needed, and whether it honestly recognized failure.

Research on agent evaluation warns that accuracy alone can hide costs and weak testing. Work on longer tasks adds evidence about reliability. [40], [39] Alongside unfamiliar interactive tasks [9], these studies help design our encounters. They do not validate a universal survival score. The story is the encounter; detailed conditions belong in the notes.
