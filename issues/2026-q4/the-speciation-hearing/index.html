The speciation hearing

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI specialization means developing a distinct, persistent role or set of capabilities. A new product name does not establish a new technical lineage or functional type. The proposed 'speciation' test asks whether differences in function, history and dependence justify a lasting category.

A new creature, or a new coat?

Every launch arrives with a name. Our field guide needs a better reason to introduce a new animal.

We will provisionally call a change "speciation" when it produces a lasting, meaningfully different combination of abilities, dependencies, or roles. A specialist that establishes a distinct niche may deserve its own portrait. A routine update can keep the familiar creature and change the relevant feature.

Current candidateOur provisional judgment
Haiku 5.5 [3]A within-family adaptation to investigate.
Gemini workplace system [5]-[6]A new arrangement of model, tools, and access to examine.
Possible Large 4 descendants [2]A watch item; an announced release plan is not evidence of descendants.

Linux / open case: Torvalds' July invitation to fork is a governance boundary, not a verified new lineage. Fork it: the right to branch follows the distinction between community disagreement and sustained independent development. [105]

No confirmed speciation event is established by this draft's limited evidence. Our three portraits identify functional characters so readers can follow the story; they do not settle that classification.

Proposed recurring feature: The Molting Plate. Show the old portrait beside the new one, keeping everything constant except the documented change. A new detachable implement would mean an added tool. A changed beak might represent a different specialization. Each change gets a plain-English caption and a dated source.

When unrelated AI families acquire similar functions, we can place their portraits side by side as an example of convergent evolution. Similar appearance should never be used as evidence of actual ancestry.
