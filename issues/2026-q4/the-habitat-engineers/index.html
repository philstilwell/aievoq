Mathematics & the habitat / 2 min read

The Habitat Engineers

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI's development is shaped by institutions as well as models. Companies, funders, governments and professional communities influence access, standards, costs and research priorities. Their decisions change the conditions under which different AI systems can be developed and used.

A beaver's dam changes the conditions in which other creatures live. Ecologists call organisms that physically reshape habitats ecosystem engineers. Related work on niche construction examines how organisms alter conditions that affect selection. The ideas overlap, but they are not identical. [58], [59]

For this magazine, the question is what people rebuild around AI. Do they change the forms, the review process, the training route, or even the questions worth asking? The mathematics dispute and the nursery problem give us places to investigate. A changed habitat may create opportunities for some inhabitants and difficulties for others.

Reserved case / follow one changed practice

Choose a research group, classroom or workplace with documented practices before and after adoption. Record who changed what, why they changed it, and whose work became easier or harder. Keep other explanations in view. A new rule appearing after an AI launch does not establish that the launch caused it.

Academic starting points: the existing studies of assisted learning and workplace assistance show different outcomes in different settings. They guide our questions about design and use; they do not yet demonstrate the broader institutional changes proposed here. [44], [46]

Reporting status: the case and its documented before-and-after comparison remain open for further reporting. There is no measured claim here that AI has redesigned an entire profession.

Where the metaphor bends: people and institutions make these changes. The metaphor does not give software an independent intention to build a habitat.
