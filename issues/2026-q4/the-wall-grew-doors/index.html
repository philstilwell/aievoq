The wall grew doors

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI capability can improve through changes to training, additional computation while answering, external tools or coordination among systems. An improvement in the complete working system need not come from a larger or newly trained model. The source of a gain matters when judging its limits.

More than one route to a better creature

Predicting a bird's future from its wings alone misses its beak, feeding strategy and flock. AI can improve through changes to both the model and the arrangements in which it works.

These routes overlap. They are not independent laws whose gains can simply be multiplied. Longer-task reliability is partly an outcome of better tools, checking and recovery, rather than a separate engine of intelligence.

Food and exercise both matter. Scaling studies examine how model size, training data and computing resources interact. Different allocations can yield different results for the same budget. [34], [35] There is no universal tenfold price for an extra unit of intelligence.

The scratchpad has a history. Intermediate reasoning steps were studied in 2022. Later experiments found that spending more effort before answering can improve performance on some problems; the useful strategy depends on the problem. [27], [36] DeepSeek's R1-Zero and R1 then explored different training recipes. Neither began without an already-trained base. [28]

The flock has a feeding bill. Research finds that teams can help on divisible tasks, while coordination and checking failures can erase their advantage. [37], [38] Anthropic's widely repeated 90.2% gain belongs to one internal research evaluation, not all agent teams. [29]

October's lesson: inspect the whole creature and its equipment. ReAct and Toolformer provide earlier demonstrations of gains from using tools; this quarter's workplace connections make that idea increasingly visible to ordinary users. [42], [43], [5]-[6]

The fifth proposed route is feedback: AI helps develop better AI. Research already tests pieces of that work, with substantial limits. [41], [51] Those results justify a watchtower, not an uninterrupted arrow to superintelligence.
