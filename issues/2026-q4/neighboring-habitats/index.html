Neighboring habitats

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI can change businesses around it by answering questions before people visit a website, helping customers complete tasks inside existing software, or replacing a tool they previously paid for. To understand these effects, we need to track website visits, revenue from sales and customer behavior separately. A change in any one measure does not, by itself, show that AI caused it.

When the river changes course

A new species can change a habitat without eating its neighbors. It may redirect a stream, supply a new food source or make an old feeding ground less useful. AI can similarly change the routes by which attention and money reach other businesses. Search engines, publishers and companies selling software by subscription over the internet, known as software-as-a-service or SaaS, occupy different places along those routes.

The recurring question is simple: where are attention and spending moving, and who is adapting? Three records will follow the evidence: visits sent to websites, search revenue and a fixed starting watchlist of software companies. These are observations of a changing habitat, not an extinction forecast.

Reading the river gauges

A referral is a visit sent from a website or app to another website. These charts count it only when the destination uses Statcounter tracking and the source of the visit can be identified.

TermMeaning and example
Search referralYou search on Google or Bing, click a result and arrive at another website. That arriving visit is the referral.
AI-chatbot referralYou ask ChatGPT or Gemini a question, click a website link in its answer and arrive at that website. That arriving visit is the referral.

Reading an answer without clicking through creates no referral visit. One person can generate several visits. The categories describe where visits came from; search engines themselves can also use AI. [115], [116], [117]

The percentages answer two separate questions. Of every 100 recorded visits sent by search engines, about 90 came from Google (89.94%). Of every 100 recorded visits sent by AI chatbots, about 80 came from ChatGPT (80.47%). The two pools may differ greatly in size. These percentages therefore cannot tell us whether ChatGPT sends nearly as many visitors as Google. Both describe September, not the whole of Q3 or Q4. [115], [116], [117]

Statcounter’s participating websites do not represent every website or country equally. A visit that cannot be identified as coming from a particular service cannot be assigned to that service here. The charts are useful route markers, with a limited field of view. [117]

The missing measure is especially consequential for publishers: what share of comparable searches ends in a visit, and what is that visit worth? A click-through rate measures how often a defined set of searches or answer views leads to a website click. A zero-click rate measures how often it ends without one. A conversion rate measures how often visits lead to a specified action, such as a purchase or signup. We will add these records when their methods support repeated comparisons. For now they remain explicit gaps, rather than numbers inferred from the referral charts.

A busy river can still change direction

Alphabet’s October 28 release reports Google Search & other revenue of $63.350 billion for July–September, against $63.271 billion for April–June: a sequential increase of about 0.1%. Its reported year-over-year growth moved from 17% to 12%, a fall of five percentage points in the growth rate. Revenue did not fall by 5%. These are Q3 results newly reported during Q4. [118], [119]

Revenue also reflects advertising prices, advertiser demand and other business conditions. Growing search revenue can coexist with changing traffic patterns; it does not establish what happened to publishers’ income. The upstream business and the downstream habitat need separate observations.

Software: competition, partnership, or both?

AI tools may substitute for a paid application, or make that application more useful. The same software company can experience both pressures. The opening watchlist follows Salesforce, ServiceNow and Adobe: established businesses serving customer management, business workflows and creative work. Their disclosures provide repeated observations, but three companies cannot represent the entire SaaS ecosystem. A fiscal quarter follows a company’s own reporting calendar; it may end in a different month from a calendar quarter. FY2027 means the company’s fiscal year 2027, which can include months in calendar 2026. Year-over-year growth compares sales with the same reporting quarter one year earlier.

The present record shows continued revenue growth at all three software companies, with different changes in pace. It does not establish that AI is harmless, that adoption has paid for itself, or that smaller competitors are thriving. Salesforce’s totals include acquired Informatica revenue; ServiceNow also described some revenue moving from Q3 into Q2. Comparisons need those explanations alongside the bars. [120], [121], [122], [123]

New plumage needs a new label

AI revenue disclosures deserve their own notebook. Annual recurring revenue, or ARR, expresses recurring business on a one-year basis under the company’s definition; it is not revenue earned in one quarter. Salesforce reported Agentforce ARR above $1.5 billion, but broadened that measure in Q2 FY2027 to include Slackbot and Headless 360. We mark a break in the series instead of presenting an uninterrupted quarterly growth line. [120], [121]

ServiceNow’s October release raised its 2026 AI annual contract value target to $1.7 billion. Annual contract value expresses contracts on a one-year basis under the company’s definition. This target is a forecast, not an achieved quarterly revenue result. Different companies’ AI labels and contract measures cannot be pooled into a single AI-sales total. [123]

The permanent field stations

Recurring recordWhat will be comparedUpdate rule
Referral routesSame provider, geography, platform and referral categoryCapture monthly; compare matching quarter-end months. First September baseline has no verified prior-quarter change.
Search businessGoogle Search & other revenue and annual growthEach earnings release; retain observation dates, release dates and seasonal context.
SaaS business healthEach named company’s revenue and annual growthConsecutive fiscal quarters; no combined score or silent changes in membership.
AI business disclosuresSame product scope and contractual measureSeparate achieved results from targets; break the series when definitions change.

Further measures will include customer retention (keeping customers), paid seats (paid user licenses), usage-based pricing (charging by how much a service is used) and margins (the share of revenue remaining after specified costs), where consistent disclosures allow it. A renewal rate is not the same as revenue retained after upgrades and cancellations. Unreported figures remain unavailable, never zero. Companies that fail, merge or stop reporting stay in the historical record so the survivors do not quietly rewrite the story.

Each release will preserve a dated snapshot, downloadable values and the source definitions. Corrections will be recorded as revisions. The metaphor suggests where to look; repeated, comparable measurements will determine what we can say.
