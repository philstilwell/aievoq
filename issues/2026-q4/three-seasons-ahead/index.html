Demand & resources / 2 min read

Three seasons ahead

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Future AI demand depends on adoption, useful results, costs and the capacity to serve users. A scenario describes what could happen under stated conditions; it is not an observed trend or a probability estimate. Different sectors can follow different paths at the same time.

Possible paths through 2027, with an eye on 2030

The evidence leaves room for substantial further adoption. The speed, depth and commercial return remain open. These are conditional scenarios informed by adoption, organizational and energy research; no probabilities are assigned. Different sectors could experience them at the same time. [70]-[75]

Possible seasonWhat would make it visible?What could follow?
The bloom spreadsCustomers renew; useful tasks multiply; benefits survive careful comparison.AI becomes ordinary infrastructure. More niches open for specialists, and affordable access matters increasingly.
The crowded waterholeDemand outpaces power, equipment, reliable service or staff able to integrate it.Delays and selective access favor those with resources. Efficiency and simpler systems become more valuable.
The thinning seasonTrials fail to renew; buyers cut spending that does not justify its total cost.Products consolidate or disappear. Useful niches can persist even while some suppliers struggle.

Jobs are part of the habitat. More AI use can change which tasks people perform and which skills they need. Usage counts alone do not reveal net jobs gained or lost. The nursery must also keep functioning: does the partnership develop the expertise it depends on? [44], [46], [72]

Recurring watch / Is the new habitat sustainable?

At year-end, look for paid renewals, useful work per customer, total cost including human review, access delays, and evidence of learning or lost capability. Record whose measure it is and what it leaves out. Each measure answers a different question; no single score captures the health of the whole habitat.

The question for the next season: will more visitors arrive, will returning users find more useful tasks, and will enough keep paying to sustain the services they choose?

The central survival question is whether a service keeps earning its place when the novelty wears off. Expanding demand can support many creatures. It does not make every creature fit for its niche.
