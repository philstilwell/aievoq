Reading the probability chart. Values are calculated as p raised to the number of steps. Each step has the same independent probability of success; every step is required and no recovery is allowed. At 100 steps, 0.99 to the power 100 is about 36.6%, and 0.999 to the power 100 about 90.5%. Real agents can check, retry and fail in correlated ways. These lines are an illustration, not fitted AI performance or a forecast.

Sources: a reader's research map

Two sources should mean two lines of evidence

This issue's main concepts now draw on multiple research papers. Agreement can be partial; contrary findings belong in the story. A video, a company blog and a newspaper repeating the same experiment still count as one underlying study.

ConceptResearch to begin withWhat the evidence supports
More routes to capabilityScaling [34], [35]; reasoning [27], [36]Resource allocation and problem-solving methods can matter. No single law guarantees unlimited progress.
Tools extend reachReAct [42]; Toolformer [43]External tools can improve selected tasks. These experiments do not test October's products.
Teams have tradeoffsKim [37]; Cemri [38]Coordination can help or hinder, depending on the task and system.
Reliability and cost matterKwa [39]; Kapoor [40]A high score is incomplete evidence of practical usefulness. No general survival probability follows.
AI helps with AI researchRE-Bench [41]; PaperBench [51]Some research tasks can be automated to a degree. A self-sustaining development cycle is a stronger claim.
Assistance changes learningBastani [44]; Shen [45]; Brynjolfsson [46]Risks and benefits differ by task, tool design and use. Long-term professional outcomes remain open.
Mathematical contributionAlphaGeometry [49]; Binder [16]Different systems and partnerships can contribute. Correctness of each new result needs its own assessment.

How to read the notes. Peer-reviewed means other researchers assessed the paper before publication; it does not make the findings final. A preprint is a public research manuscript whose review status is not established here. Company measurements remain company measurements, even when written as a paper.

The species, their anatomy and our speciation criteria are editorial devices. Future envelopes are forecasts. Neither is presented as an empirical conclusion established by the papers. Single-source reports of particular events remain explicitly attributed and cannot carry a broad conclusion on their own.

Sources: routes to capability

Research beneath the doors and the flock

[35] Jordan Hoffmann and colleagues. Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models. Research paper, 2022. Tests the balance between model size and training data under a computing budget. Paired with Kaplan [34]; different prescriptions are not proof of a universal growth law. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556

[36] Charlie Snell, Jaehoon Lee, Kelvin Xu and Aviral Kumar. Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters. 2024 paper; conference version at ICLR 2025. Experiments show that the value of additional work before answering depends on the problem and strategy. This is not evidence that longer answers always mean better reasoning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314

[37] Yubin Kim and colleagues. Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems. Preprint first submitted December 2025; version 3, April 8, 2026. Controlled comparisons find gains and losses depending on coordination and task structure. We use the conditional finding, not a universal team-improvement multiplier. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v3

[38] Mert Cemri and colleagues. Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? Research manuscript, version 3, October 26, 2025. Analyzes system-design, coordination and task-checking failures. A study of failures is not a prevalence estimate for every deployed AI team. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657v3

[39] Thomas Kwa and colleagues. Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks. NeurIPS 2025; manuscript version 4, July 10, 2026. Defines task difficulty using human completion time at a specified AI success threshold. Its experiments and extrapolations are limited by the task collection. The later dashboard [10] is the same research lineage, not independent corroboration. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499v4

[40] Sayash Kapoor and colleagues. AI Agents That Matter. Research paper, July 2024. Examines costs, weak evaluation practices and problems transferring benchmark success to useful applications. Helps motivate our measurement choices; does not validate the proposed ecological scoring system. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502

Sources: tools and research assistants

A useful component is not a complete autonomous scientist

[41] Hjalmar Wijk and colleagues. RE-Bench: Evaluating frontier AI R&D capabilities of language model agents against human experts. Research manuscript, November 2024; version 2, May 27, 2025. Compares agents and people on seven research-engineering environments. Relative performance depends on time budget. Overlaps the METR research family and some tasks in [39], so those two are not independent replications. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114v2

[42] Shunyu Yao and colleagues. ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models. ICLR 2023. Combines intermediate reasoning with actions and access to outside information. Selected tasks demonstrate benefits; the paper does not establish general-purpose reliability or validate a current commercial assistant. https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629v3

[43] Timo Schick and colleagues. Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools. Research paper, February 2023. Trains a model to choose and use tools including a calculator and information services. Training is part of this method: do not cite it as proof that every tool improvement leaves the model unchanged. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761

[51] Giulio Starace and colleagues. PaperBench: Evaluating AI's Ability to Replicate AI Research. OpenAI-led research manuscript, April 2025, version 3. Evaluates attempts to reproduce twenty papers, grading component tasks. Partial replication is not novel discovery or autonomous creation of a successor model. The study uses AI grading, with a separate evaluation of the grader. Paired with the separately developed RE-Bench [41], not treated as an independent audit of OpenAI. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848v3

Date discipline. These studies provide background for questions raised in Q4. Their older model scores are not current capability ceilings. Neither their achievements nor their limitations determine when fully autonomous research will arrive. Our E-03 envelope concerns a future public demonstration and remains a provisional editorial forecast.

Sources: the nursery of expertise

Today's output and tomorrow's competence

[44] Hamsa Bastani and colleagues. Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. PNAS, June 25, 2025. Peer-reviewed field experiment in one Turkish high school, with nearly a thousand students. Unrestricted assistance improved assisted practice but reduced subsequent unaided performance; a teaching-oriented design largely mitigated that loss. Short educational setting, not a career-length study. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122

An August 2025 correction concerns an author's affiliation, not the results. Correction checked: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2518204122

[45] Judy Hanwen Shen and Alex Tamkin. How AI Impacts Skill Formation. Anthropic research preprint, January 28, 2026; version 2, February 1. Randomized experiments with developers learning an unfamiliar programming library found weaker understanding with AI assistance on average. The study measured learning over about an hour, not a career. Some interaction patterns involving active thinking preserved learning; these patterns are exploratory observations, not separately randomized teaching interventions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245v2

[46] Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond. Generative AI at Work. Quarterly Journal of Economics 140(2), 889-942, 2025. Peer-reviewed study of an assistant's staggered introduction among 5,172 support agents. Benefits were strongest among less experienced and lower-performing workers; the authors also report evidence consistent with learning. One workplace, a different task and study design from [44]-[45]; this is a useful counterweight, not a direct replication. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044

[47] Dylan Davis. AI Just Broke the Way People Become Experts. YouTube commentary, October 21, 2026, 12:33. Title, creator, date and duration checked on the video page. The fuller user-supplied passage received October 27 develops the apprenticeship and delegation argument; it is retained with the research notes and has not been checked word-for-word against the audio. Neither is academic corroboration. This is separate from the 15:14 scaling transcript [26]. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OmCPS7h08pY

What remains open. These studies support a conditional account of learning. They do not demonstrate society-wide loss of expertise, prove every AI tutor beneficial, or forecast employment. Workplaces' responses and longer-term outcomes remain reporting questions.

Sources: emergence and understanding

The measuring stick and the mathematical web

[48] Jason Wei and colleagues. Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models. Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2022. Describes abilities absent in smaller models but present in larger ones. Read alongside Schaeffer and colleagues [8], who challenge the interpretation of some apparent discontinuities through scoring effects. These papers disagree in emphasis; citing both does not manufacture consensus. https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682v2

[49] Trieu H. Trinh, Yuhuai Wu, Quoc V. Le, He He and Thang Luong. Solving olympiad geometry without human demonstrations. Nature 625, 476-482, 2024. Peer-reviewed AlphaGeometry paper: a learned proposal system works with a symbolic geometry engine. Olympiad geometry is a bounded domain, distinct from the open research problems of October 2026. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06747-5

[50] Jeremy Avigad. Understanding, formal verification, and the philosophy of mathematics. Author manuscript dated August 23, 2010. Scholarly philosophical analysis of proof, explanation and mathematical understanding, not an experiment measuring AI's social effects. Paired with the advisory group's present-day position [12] and the concrete research examples [16], [49]. https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/avigad/Papers/understanding2.pdf

The multiple-source rule. Major explanatory concepts need at least two substantive sources, preferably independent research teams and actual papers. Where evidence conflicts, say so. Where only one empirical example exists, report it as that example; do not promote it into a general law. A source's institutional prestige, a long reference list or a journal label cannot substitute for relevance to the exact claim.

What this draft has not done. We have checked the cited reports and their stated scope, not rerun their experiments or independently verified mathematical proofs. Several research manuscripts remain preprints. The research map is an editorial record of support and limits, not a systematic review of every paper in these fields.

Sources: teaching and dependence

Biological roots of the new field notes

[52] Alex Thornton and Katherine McAuliffe. Teaching in wild meerkats. Science 313, 227-229, 2006. Observational and experimental evidence of adults facilitating pups' acquisition of prey-handling skills. Supports the biological teaching example, not a claim that human workplaces or all animals learn in the same way. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1128727

[53] Nigel R. Franks and Tom Richardson. Teaching in tandem-running ants. Nature 439, 153, 2006. The leader and follower exchange signals during travel to food. A separate species and study supporting the broader point that assistance can transmit useful skills or information. The follower is not a juvenile: the analogy concerns teaching, not an ant nursery. https://doi.org/10.1038/439153a

[54] J. Jeffrey Morris, Richard E. Lenski and Erik R. Zinser. The Black Queen Hypothesis: Evolution of Dependencies through Adaptive Gene Loss. mBio 3, e00036-12, 2012. Hypothesis article explaining how loss of costly functions may be favoured when their benefits remain available from neighbours. A proposed evolutionary explanation, not a direct study of human skill loss. https://doi.org/10.1128/mBio.00036-12

[55] Samay Pande and colleagues. Fitness and stability of obligate cross-feeding interactions that emerge upon gene loss in bacteria. The ISME Journal 8, 953-962, 2014; published online November 28, 2013. Experiments used engineered E. coli partners that exchanged amino acids. Some partnerships gained a growth advantage in the tested setting. This is distinct experimental evidence for benefits of metabolic dependence, not proof of every mechanism or prediction in [54]. https://doi.org/10.1038/ismej.2013.211

What crosses the analogy. Assistance can transmit a capability; an outside supplier can also make self-sufficiency less valuable. What does not automatically cross is the mechanism, timescale, reversibility or outcome. Animal teaching, microbial gene loss, individual learning and organizational choices remain different processes.

Two sets of evidence. The biological comparisons use [52]-[55]. Claims about human learning draw separately on [44]-[46], with automation research [56]-[57]. The nursery and borrowed-metabolism labels are this report's editorial interpretations. These older studies are background to the October discussion, not new Q4 discoveries.

Sources: keeping judgment alive

Automation, practice and a proposed field test

[56] Lisanne Bainbridge. Ironies of automation. Automatica 19(6), 775-779, 1983. Scholarly analysis of industrial automation, including how systems may depend on skills whose acquisition and upkeep automation makes harder. An earlier warning that informs the comparison; it is not an experiment with modern generative AI. https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8

Open paper hosted by the International Federation of Automatic Control: https://tc.ifac-control.org/4/1/newsletter/ironies-of-automation/view

[57] Zana Buçinca, Maja Barbara Malaya and Krzysztof Z. Gajos. To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-Assisted Decision-Making. Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 5, CSCW1, 2021. In an experiment with 199 participants, interventions requiring more deliberate engagement reduced overreliance relative to simple explanation interfaces, with uneven benefits and lower subjective ratings for the most effective designs. Effects were task-specific; overreliance remained. https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287 https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692

Research map extension. Teaching and transmission: [52], [53]. Conditional advantages of dependence: hypothesis [54], experiment [55]. Human acquisition of skills: [44]-[46]. Active judgment and continued competence: [56], [57], with the learning experiments providing related evidence. These are complementary findings, not repeated tests of one unified biological theory.

Transcript claims held for further reporting. The supplied passage's figures about a 35% entry-level decline, analyst intakes cut by two-thirds, and junior employment falling after AI adoption have not been independently established here. Its named-company training examples also need original documentation and corroboration. They are not used as report findings. A hiring change would not by itself prove that AI caused it or that learning declined.

Proposals, not results. The skill ledger and two-draft exercise are designs for future reporting. The illustrated dependency scene carries no measured probabilities. Claims that all experts must move toward strategy, that reviewing is always enough, or that jobs necessarily survive task automation are not adopted.

Sources: why evolution is a useful lens

Sources: inheritance and technological change

Several ways for an earlier achievement to travel

[64] Edward J. Hu and colleagues. LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models. International Conference on Learning Representations, 2022; manuscript first posted in 2021. Keeps the underlying model's learned settings fixed while training a smaller set of added adjustments for particular tasks. [65] Geoffrey Hinton, Oriol Vinyals and Jeff Dean. Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network. Workshop paper, public manuscript March 2015. [66] W. Brian Arthur and Wolfgang Polak. The evolution of technology within a simple computer model. Complexity 11(5), 23-31, 2006. A computer experiment constructs logic circuits from earlier components and retains useful combinations. Supports a mechanism of cumulative recombination and dependence on available building blocks. It is a simplified technology model, not an empirical study of AI firms. https://doi.org/10.1002/cplx.20130

Earlier author-hosted working-paper version, dated December 21, 2004: https://sites.santafe.edu/~wbarthur/Papers/AP-Complexity.pdf

Separate the routes. Retained settings [64], teaching from outputs [65], and component recombination [66] are complementary examples, not three replications of one experiment. ReAct [42] and Toolformer [43] provide additional evidence about composing models with tools. The metaphor must preserve those differences.

No hidden ancestry claims. We do not infer that a particular commercial model is descended from another because their answers look alike. Shared components, copied methods and independently similar solutions require different evidence. Model names and marketing families cannot establish ancestry by themselves.

[67] Bowen Baker and colleagues. Emergent Tool Use From Multi-Agent Autocurricula. Research manuscript, 2019, revised February 2020. Open paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2430337/

Evidence across the comparison. Dependence draws on biological hypothesis and experiment [54]-[55], alongside separate tool-use studies [42]-[43]. Habitat change draws on ecology [58]-[59] and workplace or learning evidence [44], [46]; feedback into later AI selection is a proposed investigation. Reciprocal adaptation combines the ecological framework [59] with the limited AI example [67]. Repurposing links [68] with task adaptation [64]. Historical contingency pairs the biological experiment [69] with the technology model [66].

What multiple sources do and do not establish. These are distinct lines of evidence about parts of an analogy, not repeated experimental confirmation of the whole analogy. A biological paper cannot validate an AI product claim. An AI paper cannot establish a biological mechanism. We label the cross-domain connections as editorial synthesis and leave industry-wide generalizations open.
