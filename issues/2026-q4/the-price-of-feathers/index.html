The Price of Feathers

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

The value of an AI improvement depends on what it delivers and what it requires: money, computation, time and human checking. Comparing these trade-offs helps establish whether a stronger result is also a useful improvement for a particular job.

Imagine the Ledgerfinch returning with a magnificent new plume and a larger feeding allowance. Before applauding, the naturalist asks what the extra feathers help it do. This department will examine what a new AI ability delivers and what supporting it requires.

Work on additional computation before answering finds that its value depends on the problem and method. Research on agent evaluation argues for comparing cost alongside accuracy. These are reasons to measure the whole job, rather than assume that a more elaborate system is a better bargain. [36], [40]

These are candidate questions, not findings that every improvement carries every listed cost. A well-designed system may improve both quality and efficiency.

Reporting status: select a dated before-and-after case, or two systems doing comparable work. Record total charges, elapsed time, retries and human review separately. No cost-adjusted winner is named yet.

Where the metaphor bends: the plumage is a memory aid. We are not claiming that AI features arise through biological mate choice, or that appearance measures capability.
