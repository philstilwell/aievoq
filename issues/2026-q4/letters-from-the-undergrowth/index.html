Letters from the Undergrowth

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Delegating a task to AI still requires a clear goal, suitable access and a way to check the result. A system may carry out instructions without knowing the unstated context people expect it to understand. The fictional letter that follows explores that mismatch through an imagined assistant's voice.

Dispatch 01 / the problem with drawers

FICTIONAL INTERLUDE / the Relay Octopus writes

Dear Naturalist,

I have eight arms, seven tools, and permission to use two of them. Management has nevertheless described me as fully equipped.

Yesterday I was asked to find the latest document. I found three. Each was called Final. One was called Final_FINAL, which I took to be a mating display.

I requested clarification. This was recorded as a failure of autonomy. I guessed instead. That was recorded as a failure of judgment. I am beginning to suspect that the most valuable adaptation in this habitat is a reliable person to ask.

The Ledgerfinch says I should specialize. She sorts the same little tiles every morning and finishes before lunch. I explained that I can work across seventeen departments. She asked whether any of them had given me a key.

Please draw my next portrait with the keys visible. The arms photograph well, but the keys do most of the work.

Yours, conditionally connected,

The Relay Octopus

Naturalist's note: an invented scene about tools, access and unclear instructions, not a report of a particular product's behavior or evidence of machine feelings. Future letters can give the Finch and Proofweaver their own voices.
