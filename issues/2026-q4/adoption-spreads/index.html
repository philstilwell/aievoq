The quarterly census / 2 min read

Adoption spreads

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI adoption is the share of a defined population that reports using AI. Here the population is U.S. employer businesses. Recent use and plans for future use answer different questions; neither directly measures how intensively AI is used or how much value it produces.

Quarter figures are selected reference windows, not averages. The latest window, September 21–October 4, spans two quarters. History uses the October workbook; expectations are not outcomes.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Business Trends and Outlook Survey; October 22, 2026 release, October 27 workbook. [86]

The clearest long-running question is also the most ordinary: how many businesses say they are using AI? The Census series now gives a trail across the year, rather than a single crowded-waterhole sighting. [86]

The historical comparison: recent use is 4.7 percentage points higher in the selected Q3 window than in the Q1 window. These are the final reference windows wholly within each quarter, reconstructed from the October workbook. They are snapshots, not averages for the whole quarter; the latest window straddles Q3 and Q4. [86]

Expectations keep their own line. The copper series asks about use within six months. It is a statement of intent, not a realized future result. Keep future expectations beside actual use, then revisit them without assuming the same firms answered each wave.

The ruler changed before this trail began. The survey broadened its question in November 2025. Earlier figures belong to a separate series. Different surveys also count different populations: businesses, workers and employment-weighted businesses are not interchangeable. [71], [72], [92]
