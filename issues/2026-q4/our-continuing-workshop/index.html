Our continuing workshop

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

Keep the story curious, concrete, and readable

The audience is curious, casual and hungry for numbers. Statistics form the continuing record; the evolutionary setting makes the changes memorable. Technical detail earns its place when it explains a number or its implications.

The visual language is established. Three AI species, the fictional working human and eight public-figure caricatures now share ink and watercolor on warm ivory. Each has a recognizable silhouette and meaningful details. The species represent functions; the people shape the conditions around them. We can reuse both casts beside dated events, in charts and in later comparisons.

Mathematics is now a central storyline. The narrative follows the release of AI-produced work, community resistance, institutional support, a human research follow-up, and arguments over access. Future revisions should track what becomes accepted knowledge, not simply the number of claims announced.

Expectations now have their own record. We distinguish past predictions from assumptions, connect current surprises to dated events, and preserve future forecasts with outcomes that could disprove them. The transcript's speaker identification remains an open reporting task.

Start with the AI concept. Every article opens with a framed explanation of its subject in ordinary language. Define the idea and why it matters before asking the reader to interpret its biological counterpart. Article length remains flexible during development; typography stays readable.

Five statistical departments now recur. Adoption and employment begin with historical series. Capability and prices begin with dated baselines. Energy follows annual estimates and forecast revisions. Every edition preserves its data, methods and source dates; the website supplies charts and downloads.

The eventual book should preserve changing judgments. Keep earlier portraits, predictions, and classifications dated. Later chapters can revisit them without quietly rewriting what we thought at the time. Readers should see the surprises as they happened.
