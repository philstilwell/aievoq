The Proofweaver

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 28, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI-assisted mathematics can involve proposing a result, constructing a proof and checking that proof. These are different activities: a plausible argument is not necessarily correct, and a verified proof may still need a human-readable explanation. The Proofweaver helps keep those stages distinct.

The Proofweaver explores a space of possible arguments. Its web represents steps that must fit together. The loose amber strand is an idea still being tested. The separate checking frame represents software that can verify a precisely written argument; it is outside the creature because checking and proposing are different jobs.

This issue's example: mathematical work produced with AI, including OpenAI's internal research system. [1] The character represents a role, not a named public model. Other AI families can enter the same habitat.

Field question: can people check the web, understand its structure, and use it to build something further?

Keep recognizable: slate-blue shell, copper chevrons, geometric silk, and the unfinished amber thread. The portrait will recur whenever a claim moves from proposed result toward shared understanding.
