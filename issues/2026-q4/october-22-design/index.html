The elephant remembers; the goldfish asks why

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI-assisted software design can separate remembering a project's history from reviewing its plans. One AI session helps develop a specification; fresh sessions read it without the earlier conversation and challenge what is missing. The aim is to catch unclear decisions before they become software defects.

On October 22, Google announced the open-source Elephant-Goldfish Model plugin. Its launch describes a context-rich "Elephant" session for exploring and documenting a design, followed by fresh "Goldfish" sessions that check understanding, challenge weaknesses and assess readiness to implement. Google reports daily internal use by thousands of employees; that is an attributed adoption claim, without a precise count or a controlled estimate of improvement in the announcement. [93]

The Goldfish is not empty-headed. It receives the plan and relevant material, but not the conversation that produced them. A decision that seemed obvious after twenty exchanges must now stand on its own. The project's documentation also distinguishes substantial design work from a small, clear repair that can use an ordinary coding workflow. [94]

Different roles, a shared habitat

Imagine the Relay Octopus arriving with a colleague who missed every planning meeting. "You know what we meant" will not open this colleague's cupboard. The instructions must actually say where the key is.

The biological connection is division of labor and complementary roles: retaining a history and inspecting a plan without that history may serve different needs. These are arrangements within an AI-assisted system, potentially using the same underlying model. The animal names are memorable role labels, not zoological claims about memory or evidence of a new technical lineage. This belongs beside cooperation and habitat construction in the evolutionary account; it does not yet earn a speciation verdict.

What the research supports

In Lost in the Middle, Liu and colleagues found that the tested language models could miss relevant information depending on where it appeared in a long input. That helps explain why possessing a large conversation history is different from using it reliably. This 2024 study is background, not a test of the new plugin or a diagnosis of every current model. [95]

Cemri and colleagues' 2025 study of cooperating AI agents identified failures involving system design, coordination and checking. Its lesson is that adding participants also creates relationships that can fail. The study did not evaluate Elephant-Goldfish. Together, these separate research teams establish reasons to examine information handling and review carefully; they do not establish that this particular arrangement wins. [96]

Fresh context is not independent judgment. Reviewers using the same model can still share weaknesses. Nor can a fresh reader recover a requirement omitted from every document. This is a reason to demand tests against real requirements and human scrutiny, rather than count agreement among reviewers as proof. The repository explicitly says its reviews do not replace testing or human judgment. [94]

Its place in this quarter

The mathematical upheaval raises the question of how to check a growing supply of proposals. This design workflow addresses a related bottleneck in software, but review of a specification is not a mathematical proof. The demand story asks whether use persists; enthusiasm for a plugin cannot answer that. The working-human story asks whether people retain judgment; approving an AI-generated plan does not by itself demonstrate learning. The operating-cost story must count every review round and human intervention, not just the price of the first answer.

These connections make the release a useful October sighting. Whether it improves the habitat remains a measurable question.

The next sighting needs a denominator

A worthwhile comparison would use matched tasks and the same model: ordinary assistance versus design plus fresh review. Track independently confirmed defects per completed task, false alarms, total cost per accepted result, elapsed time and human checking minutes. Keep the tests and budgets fixed across quarters, and record any changes. The release supplies no comparable Q3-to-Q4 outcome series. For now, those readings are not available, rather than zero or an invented success rate.
