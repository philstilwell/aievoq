The next season

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI forecasts are dated, testable expectations about future adoption, capabilities and business outcomes. A useful prediction says what will happen, by when, and what evidence will decide it. A probability expresses uncertainty; it is not an observed statistic.

The naturalist must eventually look beyond the tracks already in the mud. Which feeding grounds will fill? Which established residents will hold their territory? Which new abilities will matter enough to spread?

Next quarter / Q1 2027

Q01 / More visitors at the watering hole

Prediction / 65% editorial probability: At least 27% of U.S. employer businesses will report recent AI use in the final Census reference window wholly within Q1 2027.

Starting point: 24.6% in September 21–October 4, 2026, from the October 22 release; this window crosses quarters. The final wholly within-quarter readings were 19.1% for Q1, 21.7% for Q2 and 23.8% for Q3 2026. These are snapshots, not quarter averages. [86]

Why this direction: The earlier readings suggest adoption is spreading. A 27% threshold asks whether that direction continues at a modest pace. What could overturn it: Trials may stall, budgets may tighten, or businesses may find less recurring value than expected.

The test: Use the Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey national estimate for AI use in the last two weeks, retaining the current employer-business population and question. Select the final reference window wholly inside January–March 2027. Yes at 27% or above; no below. The published point estimate decides the event; report its standard error separately. Missing data or a changed definition is unresolvable. Evidence cutoff: 2027-05-31.

Q02 / The forager learns to recover

Prediction / 75% editorial probability: At least two of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google/DeepMind, Meta and Mistral will publish qualifying new agent-reliability reports during Q1 2027.

Starting point: October release notes already emphasize agents and computer-based work. The existing E-02 pilot asks a related question about the remainder of 2026; it is a separate forecast with a different window. [3], [5], [7], [39], [40]

Why this direction: As agents handle longer sequences, recovering after an error and checking completed work become useful selling points. What could overturn it: Providers may emphasize other capabilities, publish no usable comparison, or describe an aspiration rather than a new result.

The test: Count each named provider once for a dated January–March 2027 release note or technical evaluation concerning a newly released model or agent version. It must explicitly report improvement over an identified earlier version in error recovery, preserving task state, or checking completed work. Google and DeepMind count as one. Reposts and vague promises do not count. Yes with at least two qualifying providers, no with fewer after reviewing the public record. This measures published claims, not independent confirmation of performance. Evidence cutoff: 2027-04-30.

Q03 / The old river still carries trade

Prediction / 75% editorial probability: Alphabet will report positive year-over-year growth in Google Search & other revenue for the quarter ending March 31, 2027.

Starting point: The recorded Q3 2026 Search & other revenue was $63.350 billion, with 12% reported annual growth, versus 17% growth in Q2. These are company figures. [118], [119]

Why this direction: An incumbent can retain a growing business while users experiment with alternative ways to find answers. What could overturn it: Advertising weakness, changing discovery habits or pricing pressure could outweigh that resilience.

The test: Use Alphabet’s Q1 2027 results, comparing Google Search & other revenue with the same release’s comparable Q1 2026 figure. Yes if revenue is strictly higher, no if equal or lower. A discontinued or incompatible measure, or no eligible release by cutoff, is unresolvable. The test concerns company revenue, not AI’s causal effect. Evidence cutoff: 2027-05-31.

Next year / calendar 2027

Y01 / More channels through the delta

Prediction / 60% editorial probability: ChatGPT’s share of worldwide, all-platform AI-chatbot referrals in Statcounter will be below 70% in December 2027.

Starting point: The September 2026 snapshot was 80.47%, retrieved October 30. It is a share of tracked, identifiable visits sent by AI chatbots to websites, not a share of chatbot users or questions. [116], [117]

Why this direction: More competing assistants could distribute outbound visits across a wider set of services. What could overturn it: A leading assistant could retain its advantage; tracking changes could also distort the apparent distribution.

The test: Use Statcounter’s December 2027 worldwide, all-platform AI-chatbot referral share after the 45-day revision window, captured by March 31, 2028. Yes below 70%; no at or above. Retain the same source and denominator; an incompatible definition or unavailable observation is unresolvable. A falling share can accompany rising absolute visits. Evidence cutoff: 2028-03-31.

Y02 / A larger year-round population

Prediction / 65% editorial probability: At least 35% of U.S. employer businesses will report recent AI use in the final Census reference window wholly within Q4 2027.

Starting point: The same 24.6% September 21–October 4, 2026 observation anchors Q01. This annual forecast and Q01 follow the same adoption process; they are related bets, not independent evidence. [86]

Why this direction: Wider availability and changes to everyday workflows could make recurring use more common over a full year. What could overturn it: The easier uses may already be adopted; integration, trust and cost could slow the next wave.

The test: Apply Q01’s Census population, question and point-estimate rule to the final reference window wholly inside October–December 2027. Yes at 35% or above; no below. Use evidence published by March 31, 2028. Missing data or a changed definition is unresolvable. Evidence cutoff: 2028-03-31.

Y03 / Some old residents adapt

Prediction / 75% editorial probability: At least two of Salesforce, ServiceNow and Adobe will report positive year-over-year total revenue growth for their last fiscal quarter ending within calendar 2027.

Starting point: The latest recorded annual growth rates were 11% for Salesforce’s quarter ending July 31, 20.5% for ServiceNow’s quarter ending September 30, and 13% for Adobe’s quarter ending August 28, 2026. Their reporting calendars differ. [120], [121], [122], [123], [124], [125]

Why this direction: Established software businesses can add AI, retain distribution and sell new capabilities while competitors attack parts of their market. What could overturn it: Price pressure, fewer paid accounts, weaker spending or new substitutes could offset those advantages.

The test: For each of the three named companies, select its last fiscal quarter with an end date in calendar 2027. Compare reported total revenue with the same release’s prior-year comparable. Yes if at least two are strictly higher; no otherwise. All three compatible releases must be public by March 31, 2028, or this forecast is unresolvable. Acquisitions must be disclosed; growth does not establish an AI benefit or describe the whole software sector. Evidence cutoff: 2028-03-31.

A window for events, a window for evidence

The outcome deadline defines when something must happen. The later evidence cutoff allows time for surveys and company results to be released; it does not grant extra time for the event itself. Preserve the source version used at review; only publications dated on or before the cutoff qualify. For a measure with a revision window, follow the forecast's stated capture rule. Data arriving late stays visible but cannot silently change the rule.

For survey thresholds, crossing the line means the published estimate crossed it; sampling uncertainty still belongs beside the result. A changed definition or missing observation is unresolvable, not a failed forecast. A public-report forecast concerns what was disclosed, not what might have happened privately. A revenue forecast concerns a business outcome, not proof that AI caused it.

These are deliberately a small, mixed set: adoption, agent reliability, discovery and the fortunes of established businesses. The two adoption calls are related, and several outcomes may move together. Six forecasts do not constitute six independent experiments. The earlier E-01 to E-03 envelopes remain a separate October pilot; their original text and lack of numerical probabilities are preserved.

The permanent register at https://aievoq.com/forecasts/ retains IDs, dates, conditions and downloadable records. Revisions should sharpen the next prediction while leaving the original available for judgment.
