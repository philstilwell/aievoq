The feeding bill

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Operating prices are the charges for using an AI system. A fixed basket of billed input and output makes published text-processing rates easier to compare over time. It measures the price of that quantity, while usefulness and the cost of completing a real job require separate checks.

Cheap output and good value are different questions. A recurring price basket makes the first easier to follow, while leaving room to investigate the second.

A token is a piece of text used for billing. The basket contains one million billed input tokens and 250,000 billed output tokens, spread across prompts no larger than 100,000 tokens. At the published standard rates, it costs $0.225 with Haiku and $2.250 with Terra. These are calculated charges, not bills from an experiment. Rates checked October 28. [4], [7]

What the basket leaves out: caching and batch discounts, tools, taxes, retries and human checking. The same volume can produce different quality, and some systems use additional billed reasoning tokens. The basket fixes the total billed quantity; it does not estimate a complete job's cost.

Quarterly rule: follow these exact models and pricing tiers. Add new entrants alongside them; mark retired or unavailable models as unavailable, never as free. A second dated capture creates a price change. Today's two-model comparison is an initial cohort, not a market-wide price index.

The companion question is cost per usable result. That belongs beside quality, time and human review in a matched task study. Different experiments cannot be stitched into a cheap-looking trend merely because they all report dollars. [40]
