New tricks and unexpected turns

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

An AI capability gain is a newly demonstrated ability or a measured improvement over an earlier baseline. To identify a gain, specify the task, conditions and role of any added tools. A striking example alone does not establish a dependable new capability.

Our phrase "gain of function" means a newly demonstrated or substantially improved ability. It is an evolutionary borrowing. For each entry, name the earlier baseline and what changed.

The most entertaining entry may be an unexpected use of an existing ability: a creature discovers a new food source with a beak it already had. Evolutionary biologists call that kind of repurposing exaptation. In AI, we can ask whether a capability built for one task proves useful for another.

Surprise deserves a small honesty check. Was the result unexpected by its developers, by independent observers, or just by us? This first issue has no earlier forecast ledger, so its surprise labels are qualitative. The new expectation field notes preserve three proposed forecasts for later review.

Even apparent sudden leaps have a research debate behind them. One team described abilities emerging with scale; another showed that some seeming jumps depend on how tests award points. [48], [8] Our field guide should show both the new behavior and the measuring stick, without declaring every leap either miraculous or illusory.

Proposed recurring feature: Strange Adaptation of the Quarter. One well-supported, unexpected behavior; a short explanation of why it surprised people; and one illustration showing its possible significance. Add failed repetitions when they occur. A memorable anecdote becomes more useful when readers know whether it is typical.
