Humans, learning & work · October 21 / 2 min read

The nursery problem

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Human expertise develops through practice, feedback and learning to recognize mistakes. AI can help people complete work, but that does not establish that they acquired the underlying judgment. The question is which kinds of assistance support learning and which bypass useful practice.

The colony is eating better than ever. The young have stopped learning how to forage. These statements could both be true. That is the uncomfortable possibility raised by Dylan Davis's video about AI and expertise, and by the fuller passage supplied for this workshop. [47]

Nature sometimes makes room for a lesson. Wild meerkats give pups opportunities to handle prey. In another study, an experienced ant guided a follower toward food, with signals between them regulating the journey. [52], [53] Feeding another animal and helping it acquire a skill are different achievements. Our workplace analogy begins with that distinction.

If the Relay Octopus sorts every document and drafts every answer, the office may become wonderfully productive. But where does a newcomer learn which detail is suspicious, why an argument fails, or when a polished answer misses the point? The danger we want to investigate is a bottleneck in the supply of new experts. It could remain hidden while experienced people still do the checking.

There is evidence for the concern, and for alternatives. A school mathematics experiment found that unrestricted AI help improved assisted practice but weakened later unaided performance; a teaching-oriented version largely avoided that loss. A separate programming experiment also found weaker learning with AI help on average. [44], [45] Yet a study of customer-support workers found especially useful gains for less experienced staff, with evidence consistent with learning. [46]

The practical distinction is between help that supports learning and help that bypasses learning. The research does not establish that every routine task is educational, or that every automated task removes a necessary lesson.

Field question / Is the nursery still working?

Follow a newcomer through a real task, feedback, and a later attempt on a fresh example. Can they explain a decision, recognize an error, or work through the problem when the assistant is absent? Record improvement separately from the amount of work completed.

This is about learning within a lifetime and knowledge passed between people. It is not evidence of genetic change. The meerkats and ants give us a memorable comparison, not a biological verdict on office life.
