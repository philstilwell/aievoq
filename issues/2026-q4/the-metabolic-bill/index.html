The metabolic bill

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

Running AI requires computing equipment and electricity. To understand its resource demands, we must distinguish measured or estimated consumption from forecasts, and AI workloads from other computing. Global data-centre electricity totals include both AI and non-AI activity.

Comparison: Annual estimate and future projection, not two quarterly observations. No comparable earlier-quarter consumption figure is available in this series.

Both include non-AI computing. One terawatt-hour (TWh) is one billion kilowatt-hours. Carry the estimate with its year; compare revisions to the same forecast year separately.

Source: International Energy Agency, Key Questions on Energy and AI, 2026 executive summary. [74]

The International Energy Agency estimates 485 terawatt-hours of electricity use by data centres worldwide in 2025 and projects about 950 in 2030. A terawatt-hour is a billion kilowatt-hours. Both totals include non-AI workloads. The outlined bar is a forecast, not electricity already consumed. [74]

Keep the two records separate. The historical series follows estimated consumption by year. The outlook series follows how forecasts for the same future year change as new editions appear. A revised 2030 projection is a change in expectations, not a quarterly jump in electricity use.

Five departments, no composite survival score. Adoption, work, capability, prices and resources answer different questions. Improvements in one can coexist with pressure elsewhere. Their shared value is a consistent record that lets readers test the story.

Numbers still to earn a permanent place: AI-caused job-loss totals, sustained earnings gains, customer retention and model survival rates. These need consistent definitions and adequate coverage.

Next-quarter comparison: preserve source dates, disclose corrections and distinguish new observations from revisions. Missing data never become zeros or invented trends.
