The evolutionary lens / 2 min read

Why this is an evolutionary story

By Phil Stilwell · Reviewed October 30, 2026 · Q4 in progress

AI concept

AI development creates competing designs, reuses earlier methods and retains some approaches while abandoning others. These processes resemble variation, inheritance and selection. The comparison includes deliberate human design; it does not mean that AI models are living organisms.

The case for a jungle with engineers

The birds, octopuses and spiders earn their place in this journal through a serious resemblance. AI development produces variants, carries earlier achievements into later designs, and gives some approaches more opportunities to continue. The surrounding world helps decide what spreads. That pattern makes evolutionary thinking useful well beyond a picturesque vocabulary.

The core connection is variation, inheritance and selection. Biological populations contain differences; some differences are passed on; and those differences can affect the contribution organisms make to later generations. These are central conditions for evolution by natural selection. Research on cultural evolution asks how related processes operate when people transmit and modify knowledge and technology. [60], [61]

In AI, inheritance has several routes. A new version can retain an earlier model's learned settings, or a new model can learn from another model's responses. Researchers also transmit methods and combine existing components. These routes differ, but each can carry something forward instead of beginning again from nothing. [64]-[66]

Sometimes the resemblance is an implemented mechanism. One research method varies and selects neural networks, allowing their structures to change across generations. Another, Population Based Training, lets stronger performers supply copied settings to other members of a training population, followed by further variation. These are concrete examples from separate research teams, not evidence that every AI is trained this way. [62], [63]

People belong inside the explanation. Engineers deliberately invent variants; users and institutions help determine which are retained. Intentional design changes the process, but does not erase copying, variation or selective retention. Cultural-evolution research and a computer model of technological recombination provide a bridge between engineering and evolutionary analysis. [61], [66]

Our claim is therefore specific: many AI development processes share evolutionary mechanisms, while the wider AI world also exhibits ecological relationships worth investigating. A company rivalry is not automatically natural selection. We must identify what varies, what is transmitted and what makes a lineage persist. The metaphor earns its keep when it helps us ask those questions more precisely.
